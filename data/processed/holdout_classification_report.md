# Holdout detection - per-class precision / recall / F1 / FPR

`data/processed/holdout_classification_report.md` - generated by `PYTHONPATH=src python -m rl_ids.holdout_eval`

## 1. The problem this evaluation works around

The playbook **action mask** is a function of the hidden severity bucket and the three masks are pairwise disjoint:

| Hidden bucket | Mask (legal actions) |
|---|---|
| Normal | Monitor |
| Suspicious | Monitor, DPI, Throttle, Honeypot |
| Compromised | Monitor, Block, Isolate |

So the mask *is* the label in disguise: handing the agent the true mask makes detection trivially perfect, and removing it leaves a network whose outputs were never forced to encode the state (the mask always told it). Both negative controls are reported in Section 2.

To get an honest, non-trivial signal we evaluate what genuinely lives in the agent's **learned representation**: a logistic-regression probe is trained on the frozen 128-d penultimate layer of the Q-network, using the **train split only**, then tested on the two eval-only holdout days. A probe on the raw 17 features is the no-representation baseline.

## 2. Negative controls (why not just read the actions)

| Model | Mask supplied (circular) | Mask-free Q-argmax | Random action |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| uniform DQN | 0.999 | 0.230 | 0.299 |
| PER | 0.999 | 0.199 | 0.299 |

Mask-supplied accuracy is ~1.0 by construction (it is handed the answer); mask-free Q-argmax collapses to the majority bucket. Neither is a usable detection metric - hence the probe.

## 3. Method

- Probe: `StandardScaler -> LogisticRegression` on the frozen 128-d layer (raw 17 features for the baseline); fit on a stratified train sample (4,000/class), never on the holdouts.
- Holdouts sampled at 3,000/class; decisions average 4 env-style observations (Gaussian noise sigma=0.05, telemetry masking p=0.1).
- Observations: **clean** (scaled features untouched) and **corrupted** (noise + masking, as at training time).
- Predicted bucket = probe class; predicted-attack = bucket != Normal.
- **FPR is one-vs-rest per row**: for `Normal` it is the share of *attack* flows predicted benign (i.e. a miss rate); the operational false-alarm rate on benign traffic is the final `_benign FP rate_` row.
- Probe train accuracy: uniform DQN (clean) 0.970, uniform DQN (corrupted) 0.934, PER (clean) 0.963, PER (corrupted) 0.934, raw-17 features (no agent) (clean) 0.893, raw-17 features (no agent) (corrupted) 0.875.

## 5. Reading the results

- **holdout_infiltration (2018)** - best *agent* probe: **PER** (macro F1 0.271) vs the raw-17-feature baseline 0.284 (-0.013). Overall ranking: raw-17 features (no agent) 0.284 > PER 0.271.
- **holdout_ids2017_portscan_ddos (2017)** - best *agent* probe: **PER** (macro F1 0.556) vs the raw-17-feature baseline 0.524 (+0.032). Overall ranking: PER 0.556 > uniform DQN 0.533.

- The probe shows how much *severity information* the agent's representation carries and, crucially, whether it transfers to the unseen days/attacks.
- Attack **families** seen in training transfer even when the label string is new: the 2017 `DDoS` label is unseen, but the family appears in training as ~1.24M rows of 2018 `HOIC`/`LOIC` traffic in the `Compromised` bucket, which is why it is recalled strongly. Families with no training counterpart (`PortScan`, `Infilteration`) are not reliably recalled - a coverage/representation limit, not a tuning bug.
- The agent's *policy* remains optimal on the training distribution; this report isolates the perception/generalisation question the mask hid.
